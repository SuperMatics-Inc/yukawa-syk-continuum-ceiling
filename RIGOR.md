# Bare-moment ceiling for the local large-N saddle

## 1. Statement and scope

**PROVED, conditional on the previously proved scalar finite-band comparison.** Write \(\tau=k_BT\) and take all phonon and fermion frequencies in energy units. Normalize the bare one-band DOS \(N\) per spin and flavor to mass one. Assume it has finite second moment and finite essential height \(H>0\). Consider the translation-invariant, scalar, pairing-symmetric large-N Yukawa saddle with local, time-reversal-invariant self-energy, a harmonic onsite oscillator of energy \(\Omega_0>0\), and equations

$$\begin{aligned}
G_n&=\int\frac{N(\xi)\,d\xi}{i\omega_n-\xi-\Sigma_n},\qquad \omega_n=(2n+1)\pi\tau,\\
\Sigma_n&=a\tau\sum_m D_{n-m}G_m,\\
\Pi_j&=-b\tau\sum_mG_mG_{m+j},\qquad D_j^{-1}=(2\pi\tau j)^2+\Omega_0^2-\Pi_j,\\
\Phi_n&=a\tau\sum_mD_{n-m}\Phi_m\int\frac{N(\xi)\,d\xi}{(\omega_m-\Im\Sigma_m)^2+(\xi+\Re\Sigma_m)^2}.
\end{aligned}\tag{1}$$

Here \(a>0\) is the variance coefficient in **both** the normal and singlet kernels, \(b>0\) includes the spin trace and flavor ratio in the bubble, and any static real Hartree term is absorbed into \(\xi\). The anomalous identity assumes opposite-momentum (or time-reversed) bare states have the same energy. The normal branch is assumed causal, with finite \(D_0>0\), a nonnegative coordinate spectral representation for \(D\), and fermionic canonical spectral mass one. There is no other attractive interaction, and there is no independent pairing cutoff. At a fixed point with these regularity assumptions, no phonon or electron mass expansion has been made. The theorem concerns the *highest linear pairing instability of eligible exact N=∞ normal branches*. It is not a theorem about a competing normal saddle, finite N, BKT coherence, or an equilibrium free-energy crossing to a superconducting state discontinuous in \(\Phi\).

On every such branch, even or asymmetric,

$$ \boxed{\tau_{\rm lin}< C_\infty\sqrt{Ha}=C_\infty\sqrt{AM},\qquad M=N_{\rm ref}a,\quad A=H/N_{\rm ref}.} \tag{2}$$

Here \(C_\infty=\sqrt{g(2)}/(2\pi)\) is the prior scalar constant, rounded to 0.18272624777232952 by the existing GAMMA certificate. The coefficient is optimal in (1), even at any fixed finite positive ratio \(b/a\), when bare bandwidth, oscillator frequency and vertex can vary. The sharpness sequence is centered; the upper bound does not assume a centered branch. The sharpness statement does not assert saturation at a prescribed nonadiabatic ratio or on a specified fixed band.

## 2. Dressed sum rule and sign conventions

**PROVED.** Use the positive *coordinate* Matsubara propagator, with \(D_{\rm bare}(i\nu)=(\nu^2+\Omega_0^2)^{-1}\); signs for a Green function defined with a minus sign must be converted before using (1). Let \(\rho_+(d\Omega)\) be its positive-frequency oscillator spectral measure. Thus

$$ D(i\nu)=\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{d\sigma(\Omega)}{\nu^2+\Omega^2},\qquad d\sigma(\Omega)=2\Omega\,\rho_+(d\Omega).\tag{3} $$

The factor of two is fixed by the positive bare line \(\rho_+=\delta_{\Omega_0}/(2\Omega_0)\), not by the spin sum. Spectral positivity follows for a stable real coordinate from the Lehmann weights \(p_i-p_j\ge0\) at positive energy differences, with the two opposite difference signs combined. The hypotheses exclude an additional elastic contribution proportional to \(\delta_{j0}\), and require the static spectral susceptibility finite. In particular, the elastic contribution is not a license to insert a mismatched pairing-only zero mode.

In the bounded-DOS saddle the exclusion of an elastic term is also compatible with the bubble itself, not a dynamical ansatz. Causality of \(\Sigma\) implies \(-\Im G(z)\le\pi H\) throughout the upper half-plane, by Poisson convolution. The resulting fermion measure is therefore absolutely continuous and bounded by \(H\); the product diagonal in the bubble has no atomic contribution. As the Stieltjes construction in Section 4 shows, a positive static denominator then gives the nonnegative coordinate measure (3).

For the canonical local electron, \(|G_n|\le1/|\omega_n|\), and \((G_n)\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\). Its bubble is absolutely summable at each transfer and \(|\Pi_j|\le b\tau\|G\|_{\ell^2}^2\le b/(4\tau)\). Moreover \(\Pi_j\to0\) as \(|j|\to\infty\): truncate the two \(\ell^2\) sequences to finite support, whose shifted overlap vanishes for sufficiently large \(|j|\), and bound the residual overlaps by Cauchy–Schwarz. For the first spectral moment the weaker assumption \(\Pi(i\nu)=o(\nu^2)\) suffices; constant shifts leave it unchanged and an inertial renormalization proportional to \(\nu^2\) need not. Dyson gives \(\nu_j^2D(i\nu_j)\to1\); monotone convergence in (3) along \(|j|\to\infty\) gives \(\int d\sigma=1\). Thus the dressed phonon has the bare oscillator strength even when \(D(0)\) is strongly enhanced. Equivalently, for a canonical coordinate this is the double commutator with the kinetic energy; the electron–coordinate interaction commutes with the coordinate. For a nonunit kinetic coefficient the same argument retains its bare residue.

Define \(\alpha^2F(d\Omega)=N_{\rm ref}a\,\rho_+(d\Omega)\) and \(d\lambda(\Omega)=N_{\rm ref}a\,d\sigma(\Omega)/\Omega^2\). Then \(\lambda=N_{\rm ref}aD_0\) and \(M=2\int\Omega\alpha^2F(d\Omega)=N_{\rm ref}a\), independent of temperature. If a vertex \(g\) multiplies the creation-plus-annihilation operator instead, \(a=2\Omega_0\overline{|g|^2}\) with the appropriate flavor variance already included. For a physical force coefficient \(F\) and ion mass \(m\) this is \(\hbar^2\overline{|F|^2}/m\). The dimensions are \([D]=E^{-2}\), \([a]=[b]=E^3\), \([N]=E^{-1}\), and \([AM]=E^2\).

## 3. Reduction, branch checks and strictness

**PROVED.** Put \(u_n=\omega_n-\Im\Sigma_n\), \(\chi_n=\Re\Sigma_n\), \(s_n=u_n/(\pi\tau)\), and \(q_n=(\mathcal P_{u_n}*(N/H))(-\chi_n)\) on \(n\ge0\). Causality gives \(u_n\ge\omega_n>0\) there; reflection gives \(u_{-n-1}=-u_n\), \(\chi_{-n-1}=\chi_n\). Then \(-\Im G_n=\pi Hq_n\), and the pairing integral is \(\pi Hq_n/u_n\). With \(K_j=HaD_j\), folding (1) gives

$$ s_n=w_n+\sum_{m\ge0}(K_{|n-m|}-K_{n+m+1})q_m,\qquad s_n y_n=\sum_{m\ge0}(K_{|n-m|}+K_{n+m+1})q_m y_m,\quad y=\Phi/u. $$

The real term has the same plus transfer combination as the even pairing sum, and \(\Re G_n=-\int N(\xi)(\xi+\chi_n)/[u_n^2+(\xi+\chi_n)^2]d\xi\). The subtracted transform of the finite-band paper at \(z_n=(\chi_n+iu_n)/(\pi\tau)\) is \(\overline{G_n}/(\pi H)\) plus a real constant: \(G_n=-\int N(\xi)/(\xi+\chi_n-iu_n)\,d\xi\). Subtracting the real zeroth row gives exactly Eq. (asym-real) of the finite-band theorem, with \(x_n=\chi_n/(\pi\tau)\); its coefficient is positive. Finite DOS mass gives absolute convergence, since \(|G_n|\le1/|\omega_n|\), \(D_0<\infty\), and \(D_j\le(2\pi\tau j)^{-2}\) for \(j\ne0\). Neither DOS evenness nor an artificially fixed real shift is required. On the centered half-filled branch of an even DOS the real integral vanishes by reflection. Normal phonon feedback is quadratic in \(\Phi\): U(1) leaves \(G,\Pi,D\) neutral but \(\Phi\) charged; a term linear in \(\Phi\) in a neutral equation is forbidden. Equivalently the anomalous part of the polarization is a product of one anomalous propagator and its conjugate.

A technical issue is that the finite-band theorem as phrased for one *fixed* spectrum does not on its own give strictness for a supremum over temperatures with different spectra. The following uniform estimate supplies the missing step. For positive frequencies write \(h_n=-\Im G_n\). Normal folding and the monotonicity of \(D_j\) give

$$u_n\ge\omega_n+a\tau(D_0-D_{2n+1})h_n,\qquad |G_n|^2\le h_n/u_n. \tag{4}$$

The second inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz against the probability DOS. Reflection extends the bound to the negative half-line. On a compact temperature interval \([t_-,t_+]\subset(0,\infty)\), if \(D_0\) tends to infinity along any sequence of eligible states, (4) forces each \(|G_n|^2\to0\), uniformly in the choice of states, because \(D_{2n+1}\le(2\pi t_-)^{-2}\). The dominating free-fermion tail is summable, so \(|\Pi_0|\le b t_+\sum|G_n|^2\to0\), contradicting \(D_0^{-1}=\Omega_0^2-\Pi_0\). Thus \(\ell=HaD_0\) is bounded by some finite \(L_0\) uniformly in a compact neighborhood of \(\tau_*=C_\infty\sqrt{Ha}\), without any continuity assumption on the exact branches.

For completeness, the pointwise proof of the case-(e) theorem in `C:/dev/wt/sci-finiteband-ceiling/science/papers/finiteband-ceiling/src/main.tex` uses \(c=Ha/(2\pi\tau)^2<C_0=19/25\), its rational guards and the strict scalar condition \(\sup\operatorname{spec}\mathcal A(K)<1\). At \(\tau_*\), \(c=1/g(2)<C_0\). By Jensen, \(K_j\le \widetilde K_j(c)=c/(j^2+c/L_0)\) for all \(j\ge1\); the latter is an Einstein sequence with finite coupling \(L_0\). The scalar monotone comparison gives \(\sup\operatorname{spec}\mathcal A(\widetilde K(c_*))<c_*g(2)=1\). It is norm continuous in \(c\) by summable transfer domination. If one uses the actual moment in the proof's flat scalar gap, this uniform majorant strengthens that gap; all of the other pointwise sufficient conditions depend only on \(c<C_0\), the transfer bounds and the real normal equation. Both input inequalities therefore hold throughout some interval below \(\tau_*\); above \(\tau_*\) they hold by the usual scalar comparison. This proves the strict inequality (2), not merely exclusion exactly at the limiting temperature.

## 4. Optimality with a dynamical bubble

**PROVED.** This is a different sharpness step than imposing an externally fixed Einstein spectrum. Fix any \(r=b/a\in(0,\infty)\), energy units \(Ha=1\), and \(0<\tau<C_\infty\). Set a flat probability DOS on \([-W,W]\), \(H=1/(2W)\), hence \(a=2W, b=2rW\). It can be realized with an even dispersion \(W(2|k|/\pi-1)\), \(-\pi\le k\le\pi\), if long-range summable hopping is allowed. More generally a scaled bounded DOS continuous at a maximum at zero works by the same Poisson limit. Prescribe the *dressed* zero-transfer inverse \(\delta=W^{-1/2}\) up to the choice of energy units. At the selected temperature invert the Dyson equation by putting

$$D_j^{-1}=\nu_j^2+\delta+\Pi_0-\Pi_j,\qquad\Omega_0^2=\delta+\Pi_0.\tag{5}$$

The bare mass so obtained is fixed once the model is constructed; it is not made a function of temperature in that model. The following existence and difference estimates concern the chosen temperature only.

Let \(\mu\) be a fermionic spectral probability measure of density bounded by \(H\). For its thermal bubble, direct Matsubara summation of the two spectral resolvents (or partial fractions and the Fermi occupation \(f\)) gives

$$\Pi_j=2b\int_{E<E'}\frac{(E'-E)[f(E)-f(E')]}{\nu_j^2+(E'-E)^2}\,\mu(dE)\mu(dE'),\qquad d=E'-E. \tag{6}$$

At \(j=0\) the integrand after symmetrization is bounded by \(\sup|f'|\); the diagonal has zero joint measure because \(\mu\) has a bounded density. The minus-bubble convention in (1) fixes the sign, and exchanging \(E,E'\) gives the factor two. The difference \(\Pi_0-\Pi_j\) is nonnegative. Using both density bounds and \(\int_{\mathbb R}[f(E)-f(E+d)]\,dE=d\), obtained by integrating \(-f'\) on shifted intervals, gives

$$0\le\Pi_0-\Pi_j\le 2bH^2\int_0^\infty\frac{\nu_j^2}{\nu_j^2+d^2}\,dd=\pi bH^2|\nu_j|.\tag{7}$$

In particular the nonzero-transfer feedback vanishes pointwise as \(W\to\infty\) even at fixed nonzero bubble ratio. The static feedback is retained, not deleted.

### Causal normal fixed point for the inverse design

**PROVED.** For each \(W\), use the weakly compact convex set of even probability measures of density at most \(H\) and second moment at most \(S\), where \(S=W^2/3+a(\tfrac12\delta^{-1/2}+\tau/\delta)\). This set includes the bare DOS. Closedness of the density condition follows by integrating nonnegative continuous compactly supported tests, tightness by the second-moment cap, and its weak topology is the usual weak measure topology on this tight set. For input \(\mu\), extend (6) to a Stieltjes function \(\Pi(s)\) of \(s=\nu^2\), with \(\Pi(0)\) finite. The function \(s+\delta+\Pi(0)-\Pi(s)\) has strictly positive imaginary part for \(\Im s>0\) and is strictly positive for \(s\ge0\). Its reciprocal is Stieltjes: the Herglotz representation of its negative has support on the negative real axis, and the asymptotic \(D(s)\sim 1/s\) eliminates constant and affine terms and fixes mass one. More explicitly the Stieltjes form follows using \(D(x)>0\) for \(x>0\), its nonpositive upper-half-plane imaginary part and \(D(x)\to0\) on the positive axis. Static mass at zero is forbidden by \(D(0)=1/\delta<\infty\). Also \(\int\Omega^2d\sigma=\delta+\Pi(0)\), taking \(s(1-sD(s))\) to infinity by monotone convergence and using \(\Pi(s)\to0\). The integral \(\Pi(s)\) tends to zero by domination by the static integrand.

Form the causal self-energy via the usual product-convolution identity, stated explicitly with positive weights:

$$\Sigma(z)=a\int\!\mu(dE)\rho_+(d\Omega)\left[\frac{n_B(\Omega)+1-f(E)}{z-E-\Omega}+\frac{n_B(\Omega)+f(E)}{z-E+\Omega}\right].\tag{8}$$

Partial fractions in the bosonic Matsubara sum of \(D(i\nu_j)G(i\omega_n-i\nu_j)\) give (8) on the fermionic frequencies; using (3) fixes its sign. It is the Cauchy transform of a finite nonnegative even measure of mass at most \(a(\tfrac12\delta^{-1/2}+\tau/\delta)\), by \(n_B(\Omega)\le\tau/\Omega\), \(\int \Omega^{-2}d\sigma=1/\delta\), and Cauchy–Schwarz. The output is the spectral probability measure of \(\int N(\xi)[z-\xi-\Sigma(z)]^{-1}d\xi\). It is even, is normalized by its large-imaginary-frequency limit, and has density bounded by \(H\): the negative imaginary part is a Poisson convolution of \(\pi N\) at height \(\Im (z-\Sigma(z))>0\). The output second moment is the bare variance plus the total \(\Sigma\) weight. To see this without assuming moment finiteness, put \(z=iy\), write \(z-\Sigma(z)=i(y+d_y)\) with \(y d_y\to\operatorname{mass}\Sigma\), and split \(y^2(1-y[-\Im G(iy)])\) into the shift contribution \(y^2d_y/(y+d_y)\) and the bare resolvent deficit times \(y/(y+d_y)\). The total increases to the output second moment by monotone convergence, and the two terms have the claimed limits. Thus the map takes the set into itself.

For weak continuity, the polarization integral at fixed \(s>0\) and at zero uses bounded continuous functions of the pair \((E,E')\) after symmetrizing (6) and extending the zero-transfer quotient to its diagonal. The phonon measures have uniformly bounded second moment because \(\Pi(0)\le b/(4\tau)\); uniqueness of the Stieltjes transform implies weak continuity. The integral of \(\Omega^{-2}\) is exactly \(1/\delta\) for every measure including the limit; cutting off this integrand at a finite level and using convergence of the integrals of the clipped function gives uniform integrability at zero along every convergent sequence. These facts permit passage to the limit in (8), whose integrand against \(d\sigma\) is bounded by a constant times \(\Omega^{-1}+\Omega^{-2}\) at fixed \(\Im z>0\). Weak limits of the output are unique by their Cauchy transforms; tightness was shown above. The Schauder–Tychonoff fixed-point theorem therefore supplies an even causal normal solution. At the fixed point \(\Omega_0^2>0\) and all of (1) hold. No uniqueness or thermodynamic selection is asserted.

### Pairing witness in the limit

**PROVED.** At this normal state \(K_0=1/\delta=\sqrt W\) and \(K_j\to 1/\nu_j^2\) for every fixed \(j>0\), by (5)–(7). By folding the normal row, \(u_n\le \pi\tau(w_n+K_0+2\sum_{j\ge1}K_j)\), hence \(u_n/W\to0\) at each fixed \(n\). Consequently \(q_n= (2/\pi)\arctan(W/u_n)\to1\). The eliminated form is the one in the finite-band proof, Eq. (S); the zero transfer cancels and its diagonal tails are dominated by \(j^{-2}\). On every finite-support test it converges to the scalar \(\gamma=2\) operator times \((2\pi\tau)^{-2}\). By its spectral definition the limit has top \(C_\infty^2/\tau^2>1\). A finite-support trial already has quotient greater than one, and retains it for sufficiently large finite \(W\). For each \(\tau<C_\infty\) this yields a dressed, self-consistent, exact saddle with a linear instability at that temperature, proving sharpness of (2) over the stated model family.

## 5. The delta DOS is not a bounded DOS

**PROVED (a nonsharp dimensionally natural bound).** In a dot with unit atomic DOS, the normalization \(H\) above does not exist. Nevertheless at positive temperature the Poisson-window height is at most \(1/(\pi^2\tau)\), since \(u_n\ge\pi\tau\); this holds also at nonzero chemical potential on an eligible reflected branch. In the dimensionless normal and pairing equations use the finite temperature height \(1/(\pi^2\tau)\) as normalization, so \(0<q_n\le1\) and \(K_j\le a/[\pi^2\tau(2\pi\tau j)^2]\). In the normal-eliminated form, write the Toeplitz-plus-Hankel combination as the positive difference term with coefficient \(P=D-H\) and a bilinear Hankel attraction with coefficient two. Bounding the latter by \(2|y_n y_m|\le |y_n|^2+|y_m|^2\), using \(w_n\ge1\) and \(\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-2}=\pi^2/6\), proves stability for \(\tau^3>a/(12\pi^2)\). Thus \(\tau_{\rm lin}\le[a/(12\pi^2)]^{1/3}\): unbounded \(T_{\rm lin}\) at fixed natural bare moment \(a\) is not possible in this class. This rough bound neither computes a globally optimal dot coefficient nor identifies a published saturation coefficient with an exact upper bound. In particular, the \(\gamma=2\) certificate applies directly to the *constant-weight* eliminated limit of Section 4, not to an arbitrary dot with weights \(1/u_n\). Distinguish any dot asymptotic mapping from a full-frequency identity.

### A coupling-independent dot bound and its strong-coupling limit

**PROVED (centered dot, same-kernel pairing).** Write \(r=b/a\), \(h_n=1/u_n\) for \(n\ge0\), \(h_*=\max_n h_n\), \(\sigma=\sqrt{a\tau D_0}\), and \(R=a h_*/(12\tau)\). The maximum exists because \(u_n\ge\omega_n\to\infty\). The nonzero transfers in the normal equation obey

$$ |u_n-\omega_n-\sigma^2/u_n|\le 2a\tau h_*\sum_{j\ge1}D_j\le R. \tag{9} $$

The solution \(u_n^{\rm st}=(\omega_n+\sqrt{\omega_n^2+4\sigma^2})/2\) of the purely static equation satisfies \(\sigma\le u_n^{\rm st}\le \omega_n+\sigma\), and the function \(u-\sigma^2/u\) has derivative at least one. Thus \(|u_n-u_n^{\rm st}|\le R\). At a maximum of \(h_n\), (4) gives \((\sigma h_*)^2\le1+a\tau D_1h_*^2\). The static bubble is \(\Pi_0=2ra\tau\sum_{n\ge0}h_n^2<\Omega_0^2\). Estimating the square sum from below by the integral of the decreasing function \((\pi\tau(2x+1)+\sigma+R)^{-2}\) yields

$$\begin{aligned}
a h_* &\le \frac{\pi\Omega_0^2}{r}(\pi\tau h_*+\sigma h_*+Rh_*)\\
&\le \frac{\pi\Omega_0^2}{r}\left[1+\sqrt{1+\frac{\Omega_0^2}{8\pi^2r\tau^2}}+\frac{\Omega_0^2}{24r\tau^2}\right].
\end{aligned}\tag{10}$$

The Hankel form bound used above with \(K_j=(a h_*/\pi)D_j\) requires \(a h_*\ge12\pi\tau^2\) for instability. At \(\tau\ge\Omega_0/\sqrt{5r}\), (10) divided by \(\pi\tau^2\) is at most \(5(2+5/144+5/24)<12\), using \(\sqrt{1+x}\le1+x/2\) and \(\pi>3\). The same bound retains slack on a common interval below the threshold for all \(a\). Hence \(\tau_{\rm lin}<\Omega_0/\sqrt{5r}\), independent of coupling. This bound is not sharp.

**PROVED (uniform asymptotic pairing operator on eligible centered branches).** Keep \(r,\Omega_0>0\) fixed and let \(a\to\infty\). All estimates here are uniform for \(\tau\) in compact positive intervals. The bubble bound gives \(h_*=O(a^{-1/2})\), so \(R=O(\sqrt a)\). The integral lower bound in (10) before multiplication by \(h_*\) gives \(\sigma+R+\pi\tau\ge ra/(\pi\Omega_0^2)\), implying \(\sigma\gtrsim a\). In particular \(D_0^{-1}=a\tau/\sigma^2\to0\) and \(u_n/u_n^{\rm st}=1+O(a^{-1/2})\) uniformly in \(n\). Put \(t(x)=(\sqrt{x^2+4}-x)/2\), so \(1/u_n^{\rm st}=t(\omega_n/\sigma)/\sigma\). The midpoint Riemann sum of \(t^2\) converges by the integral test (it is decreasing and integrable); its integral is \(4/3\), since \(x=2\sinh v\) gives \(t=e^{-v}\) and \(dx=2\cosh v\,dv\). Hence

$$ \Pi_0=\frac{4ra}{3\pi\sigma}(1+o(1))\longrightarrow\Omega_0^2,\qquad
\frac{a}{\pi\sigma}\longrightarrow\frac{3\Omega_0^2}{4r}. \tag{11} $$

For fixed \(j>0\) the bubble difference is \(b\tau\|v-\operatorname{shift}_j v\|_2^2/2\) on the signed full line \(v_n=\operatorname{sgn}(\omega_n)h_{n\ {\rm reflected}}\). This identity uses \(\Pi_j=b\tau\sum_n v_n v_{n+j}\). For its purely static version, \(\|v^{\rm st}\|_\infty\le1/\sigma\) and its total variation is at most \(4/\sigma\); thus the squared norm of the \(j\)-difference is at most \(8j/\sigma^2\). The error \(v-v^{\rm st}\) is \(O(R/\sigma)\) relative in \(\ell^2\), and \(b\tau\|v^{\rm st}\|_2^2=O(1)\). It follows that \(\Pi_0-\Pi_j\to0\), so \(D_j\to1/\nu_j^2\). Use \(q_n=\sigma h_n\) and \(K_j=(a/\pi\sigma)D_j\) in the eliminated operator. The \(q_n\) are bounded uniformly and tend to one at each fixed \(n\), and \(K_j\) for \(j>0\) is bounded by a uniform multiple of \(j^{-2}\). Dominated convergence of the off-diagonal Hilbert–Schmidt sum and of the diagonal on each finite block, plus the uniform \(O(1/w_n)\) diagonal tail, prove operator norm convergence to \((3\Omega_0^2/(4r))(2\pi\tau)^{-2}\) times the scalar gamma-2 matrix. The uniform upper bound from (10) rules out an additional onset escaping to infinite temperature. The limsup of branch instabilities is therefore at most \(C_\infty\sqrt{3/(4r)}\,\Omega_0\). The matching liminf holds whenever eligible normal states exist at the fixed subthreshold temperatures along the chosen branch family. No uniform finite-\(a\) inequality with this limiting constant follows. At \(r=2\), the strong-coupling constant is \(C_\infty\sqrt{3/8}\). A nonnegative weakening of the pairing kernel only, by \(1-\alpha\le1\), cannot raise its top, since the original symmetrized linear pairing operator is entrywise positive.

Esterlis and Schmalian [DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.100.115132](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.100.115132), Eqs. (35)–(37), already derived the equal-flavor limiting eigenproblem and explicitly identified the Allen–Dynes factor \(\lambda_{\rm eff}=3/8\). Their normalization has \(\alpha_c=4/g(2)\), so their \(\Omega_0\sqrt{3/(8\pi^2\alpha_c)}\) is this same limit. The contributions here are convergence with full polarization and all Matsubara modes controlled, centered-state existence, the extension in \(r=b/a\), and the tight enclosure. In their flavor-count convention \(r=2/(M/N)\); \(r\) is not \(M/N\).

## 6. Sharp continuum saturation in the regularized Gnezdilov–Boyack equation

**PROVED, for the precise regularized infinite-band pairing equation** of Gnezdilov–Boyack [arXiv:2505.02894v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02894v3) / [DOI 10.1103/7zmy-xtcw](https://doi.org/10.1103/7zmy-xtcw), Eqs. (8), (11)–(16), as verified in the paperpipe brief. This is separate from the finite-band Hamiltonian statement (2). The scale \(E\) equals \(\varepsilon_F\) in the printed transfer. In the microscopic cutoff integral use \(E=E_D=k_D^2/(2m_0)\): with \(\omega_D=ck_D\), \(\nu_{\rm DOS}=m_0/(2\pi)\) and \(\lambda=\nu_{\rm DOS}g^2/(4\pi c^2)\), the damping \(2\pi\nu_{\rm DOS}^2g^2|\nu_j|\) divided by \(\omega_D^2\) is \(2\pi\lambda|\nu_j|/E_D\). Thus identifying \(E_D=\varepsilon_F\) requires \(k_D=k_F\); for independent cutoffs \(E_D/\varepsilon_F=(k_D/k_F)^2\). For \(\omega_D>0,\lambda>0\) the scalar nonzero transfers after zero-mode cancellation are

$$ K_j=\lambda\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{(\nu_j/\omega_D)^2+2\pi\lambda|\nu_j|/E}\right),\quad
F_j=\frac{E}{2\pi|\nu_j|},\qquad j\ge1. \tag{12} $$

The infinite-band closure uses a normal propagator with constant integrated DOS and a subtracted static polarization. Thus the formal \(K_0\) cancels between the gap and mass; treating it as a finite input to the finite-band theorem would be invalid. There is no independent cutoff of the pairing equation. This use of the model takes \(N\to\infty\) before testing the exact saddle and takes \(\lambda\to\infty\) only after eliminating its static term.

The elementary inequality \(\log(1+z)<z\) for \(z>0\) gives the uniform comparison

$$ K_j<\frac{F_j}{1+d j}\le \frac{F_j}{1+d},\qquad d=\frac{E\tau}{\lambda\omega_D^2}>0. \tag{13} $$

Here no asymptotic form of the logarithm has been substituted in the bound. The dimensionless ratio of the two positive denominator terms is \((\nu_j/\omega_D)^2/[2\pi\lambda|\nu_j|/E]=d j\). Let \(\mathcal A\) be the scalar eliminated operator:

$$\mathcal A(K)_{nm}=\frac{(1-\delta_{nm})K_{|n-m|}+K_{n+m+1}}{\sqrt{w_nw_m}}
-\delta_{nm}\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^nK_j}{w_n}.$$

Indeed, Eq. (11) has the difference \(\Delta_m-(\omega_m/\omega_n)\Delta_n\) and its \(m=n\) numerator vanishes before removing a common static regularization. The nonzero opposite-sign transfer \(n+m+1\) survives even on the folded diagonal. For \(y_n=\Delta_n/w_n\), the even folded equation is \(s_n y_n=\sum_m P_{nm}y_m\) with \(s_n=w_n+2\sum_{j=1}^nK_j\) and \(P_{nm}=(1-\delta_{nm})K_{|n-m|}+K_{n+m+1}\), omitting the Toeplitz diagonal. The signed normal sum giving \(2\sum_{j=1}^nK_j\) pairs the two directions of each transfer. The full symmetrized gap form has a nonnegative kernel, and taking the modulus and then reflecting a top eigenvector gives a nonzero even top eigenvector. On that sector the congruence of \(I-P_{nm}/\sqrt{s_ns_m}\) by the diagonal \(\sqrt{s_n/w_n}\) is \(I-\mathcal A(K)\); the diagonal and its inverse are bounded. Thus the unit spectral threshold corresponds to the onset.

The scalar positive-transfer monotonicity in case (e) applies to positive nonincreasing summable transfers. To compare with the nonsummable \(F_j\propto j^{-1}\), cut both sequences after \(L\), add the same \(\epsilon/j^2\) to both if strict positivity is required, and take \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), then \(L\to\infty\). All approximants are ordered and nonincreasing. Under a common \(C/j\) envelope, operator norm convergence for pointwise transfer convergence follows as follows. The off-diagonal squares are summable: for a fixed difference \(j\), \(\sum_n 1/(w_nw_{n+j})\le \sum_n w_n^{-2}\), and the difference squared has a \(j^{-2}\) envelope; the Hankel squares satisfy the same estimate, grouping by \(n+m+1\) and using \(2/(w_nw_m)\le w_n^{-2}+w_m^{-2}\). The diagonal tends uniformly to zero in the index, bounded by \(O((1+\log n)/w_n)\), and converges on finite blocks. This proves norm continuity of the limiting scalar operator with the required ordered comparison.

The top of \(\mathcal A(F)\) is \((E/(2\pi\tau)) C_1\), where \(C_1=\sup\operatorname{spec}\mathcal A(j^{-1})/(2\pi)\) is the GAMMA gamma-1 constant. At \(\tau_*=(E/(2\pi))C_1\) the factor \((1+d)^{-1}\) in (13) gives a strictly positive gap. It retains slack just below \(\tau_*\) by continuity of the formula, and also gives stability for all \(\tau\ge\tau_*\). Therefore for every finite \(\lambda\)

$$ \boxed{\tau_{\rm lin}<\frac{E C_1}{2\pi}.} \tag{14} $$

For each \(\tau>0\), \(K_j\to F_j\) as \(\lambda\to\infty\), by \(x^{-1}\log(1+x)\to1\) at \(x=0\). The \(F_j\) envelope gives operator norm convergence by the preceding proof. At every fixed \(\tau<\tau_*\) the limiting top exceeds one, so for sufficiently large \(\lambda\) there is instability at that \(\tau\); together with (14) this proves \(\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\tau_{\rm lin}=\tau_*\). Neither assertion assumes determinant monotonicity or an exchange of a Matsubara cutoff with the coupling limit. The \(\gamma=1\) limit itself was already identified in the cited paper. The GAMMA certificate and the outward interval division in `replay.py` enclose the model's exact sharp coefficient; they are not a measurement.

For comparison with (2), the same scalar equation also obeys the bare-moment bound with \(A=1\), \(M=\lambda\omega_D^2=\nu_{\mathrm{DOS}}g^2 k_D^2/(4\pi)\) in the paper's convention. Indeed \(K_j<\lambda\omega_D^2/\nu_j^2\) for \(j\ge1\), and \(\nu_j^2 K_j\to\lambda\omega_D^2\). The boson-mode factor \(k_D^2/(4\pi)\) is the normalized area integral of the hard momentum disk, not an extra spin factor. Strict first-transfer scalar comparison and norm continuity give strict stability on a neighborhood of and above the bare-moment threshold. This last step uses only nonzero transfers in the regularized scalar equation; no finite static coupling of the continuum massless model is asserted.

## 7. Published lattice saddle and normal-state existence

**PROVED (exact change of variables for the cited saddle).** In Valentinis, Inkof and Schmalian [DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.108.L140501](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.108.L140501), Eqs. (3)–(4), put \(v=t_0^2/2>0\), \(a=\bar g^2\), \(b=2a\) in the equal-ratio convention of the plotted results. Eliminating the random-hopping term \(vG\) gives \(G=(z-vG)^{-1}\), \(z=i\omega+\mu-\Sigma_Y\), with the canonical root equal to the Cauchy transform of the probability semicircle of radius \(2\sqrt v\) and height \(H=1/(\pi\sqrt v)\). The anomalous \(vF\) term must also be eliminated, not dropped. In their convention \(F=-|G|^2\Phi_{\rm tot}\) to linear order, and \(\Phi_{\rm tot}=\Phi_Y-vF\). Thus

$$ -F=\frac{|G|^2}{1-v|G|^2}\Phi_Y
=\frac{-\Im G}{\Im z}\Phi_Y\quad(\Im z>0), $$

the last identity following from the imaginary part of the normal quadratic. The factor is exactly the time-reversed pair integral of the same semicircular DOS. At negative frequencies use reflection. A bare chemical-potential shift belongs in the DOS argument; it does not change the height. Therefore (2) applies to this lattice with \(AM=\sqrt{2}\bar g^2/(\pi t_0)\), not \(\bar g^2/t_0^2\) or an interacting DOS value at zero. The optimality proof in Section 4 also works for scaled semicircles: replace \(W^2/3\) by the semicircle variance, keep \(H\propto W^{-1}\) and \(a=1/H\), and use \(q_n\to1\) by the Poisson approximate identity at the zero-energy maximum. This establishes sharpness for the family with varied \(t_0,\Omega_0,\bar g\), not for fixed \(t_0/\Omega_0\).

**PROVED (existence of a regular centered normal state at fixed positive temperature).** For bounded, centered, even DOS, use the spectral fixed-point map in Section 4 with \(\delta_\epsilon(\mu)=\max\{\Omega_0^2-\Pi_\mu(0),\epsilon\}\) and the variance cap with \(\delta=\epsilon\). The map stays continuous; in the proof of weak continuity the integrals \(\int\Omega^{-2}d\sigma=1/\delta_\epsilon(\mu)\) now vary continuously in \(\mu\). A fixed point exists for each \(\epsilon>0\). Were clamping active for arbitrarily small \(\epsilon\), (4) would force \(\Pi_0\to0\) since \(D_0=1/\epsilon\). But clamping requires \(\Pi_0\ge\Omega_0^2-\epsilon\). For small \(\epsilon\) the fixed point therefore has the prescribed bare \(\Omega_0\). This supplies normal-state existence, not uniqueness or selection.

For the centered dot at fixed \(\tau>0\), approach the atomic bare DOS by flat probability bands of width tending to zero, keeping \(a,b,\Omega_0\) fixed. Along the constructed fixed points, (4) bounds \(D_0\) uniformly, independently of DOS height, and (8) bounds the fermionic variance. Extract weakly convergent subsequences of the fermionic and phononic measures. For the limiting electron measure \(\mu\), the nonzero-transfer bubble has the integral (6); at zero the continuous difference quotient includes the elastic term

$$ p_{\rm el}=b\sum_E\mu(\{E\})^2[-f'(E)] . $$

Let \(\widehat\Pi(s)\) denote the nonelastic Stieltjes bubble. Since \(\Omega_0^2-\Pi_0>0\) by the uniform \(D_0\) bound, the limiting phonon spectral representation for \(s>0\) is the Stieltjes resolvent of \(s+\Omega_0^2-\widehat\Pi(s)\). Its static susceptibility \(D_+(0)\) is \(1/(\Omega_0^2-\widehat\Pi(0))\), while the Matsubara static limit \(D_0\) includes \(p_{\rm el}\). To pass to the self-energy, take weak limits on \([0,\infty)\) of the measures \(\Omega^{-2}d\sigma\) of the approximating bands. On the open positive axis the limit is \(\Omega^{-2}d\sigma_+\); the mass at zero is \(e=D_0-D_+(0)\ge0\). The total mass converges by the continuous static bubble and uniform tightness, since \(\int_{\Omega>L}\Omega^{-2}d\sigma\le L^{-2}\). In (8) the kernel against this weighted measure extends continuously to \(\Omega=0\) as \(\tau/(z-E)\); its large-\(\Omega\) tail is bounded by a constant times \(\int_{\Omega>L}\Omega^{-1}d\sigma\le L^{-1}\). Hence the limiting self-energy is a causal nonelastic self-energy plus \(a\tau e G(z)\). The limiting bare Dyson equation is \(G=(z-\Sigma)^{-1}\). If \(e>0\), then at each upper-half-plane \(z\),

$$-\Im G(z)\le 1/\Im(z-\Sigma(z))\le 1/[a\tau e(-\Im G(z))].$$

This uniformly bounds \(-\Im G\), and excludes any atom of \(\mu\). Since \(-f'(E)>0\) at every finite \(E\), any atom would have made \(p_{\rm el}>0\) and hence \(e>0\), a contradiction. Thus \(e=0\), and the limiting dot state is an eligible causal fixed point at the prescribed \(\tau\). With this existence result, the matching liminf in Section 5 holds for the highest onset over centered eligible normal states, establishing the strong-coupling asymptote without presupposing a continuous normal branch through all temperatures.

### A fixed random-hopping bandwidth does not set the strong-coupling ceiling

**PROVED (centered semicircle, fixed bare hopping and oscillator energy).** The argument of Section 5 also holds for the centered Valentinis–Inkof–Schmalian saddle. To see this directly, write \(h_n=-\Im G_n\) and \(u_n=\omega_n-\Im\Sigma_{Y,n}\) on \(n\ge0\). The centered random-hopping Dyson relation gives \(1/h_n=u_n+v h_n\). Put \(\widetilde u_n=1/h_n\) and \(\sigma^2=v+a\tau D_0\). Then \(\widetilde u_n-\omega_n-\sigma^2/\widetilde u_n\) consists of exactly the same nonzero-transfer normal terms as (9), with the same bound \(R\). At a maximizer of \(h_n\), \((\sigma h_*)^2\le1+a\tau D_1h_*^2\) still follows by dropping the other nonnegative folded normal terms. The static bubble and the difference identity are unaltered. In the eliminated pairing equation use \(y_n=\Phi_{Y,n}/u_n\), \(q_n=\sigma h_n\) and \(K_j=(a/\pi\sigma)D_j\), because the effective pair integral is \(h_n/u_n\); the normal coefficient to eliminate is \(u_n\), not \(\widetilde u_n\). Thus (10) holds unchanged. For fixed \(v\), the large-\(a\) bound \(\sigma\gtrsim a\) now implies \(D_0^{-1}=a\tau/(\sigma^2-v)\to0\). All Riemann-sum and operator-norm arguments then give the same limit (11), and centered normal states exist by the construction above. In particular, at any fixed \(t_0>0,\Omega_0>0\), taking \(a\to\infty\) gives \(k_BT_{\rm lin}/\Omega_0\to C_\infty\sqrt{3/8}\).

**PROVED (counterexample to a pure-bandwidth ceiling across this family).** The bare semicircle bandwidth is \(4\sqrt v=2\sqrt2\,t_0\). Fix \(\Omega_0>0\), hold each \(t_0>0\) fixed during the strong-coupling limit, then send \(t_0\downarrow0\). The supremum of \(k_BT_{\rm lin}/(4\sqrt v)\) is infinite. This does not rule out a bound involving both \(\Omega_0\) and bandwidth, or a bound with a constant that depends on their ratio. It distinguishes the finite-band lattice result from the regularized infinite-band continuum's ceiling proportional to \(\varepsilon_F\).
